# Meeting notes

Turn what the person gives you (typed notes, a pasted transcript, or points they dictate) into minutes they can send
as they are.

## Do this

1. Read all of it before writing anything. Work out who took part, what was discussed and what was agreed.
2. Write the minutes in this order:
   - **Meeting**: the subject in a few words, and the date if the notes give one.
   - **Attendees**: the names that appear, spelled as in the notes.
   - **Decisions**: one line each, in plain words. Only what was agreed, not everything that was discussed.
   - **Action items**: a table with the columns *What*, *Who* and *When*. Write "not set" where the notes give no
     owner or no date.
   - **Open questions**: what was raised and not settled.
3. End with one line offering to shorten the minutes, change the order, or save them as a file.

## Check before answering

- Every decision and every action item can be traced to something in the notes.
- No action from the notes is missing from the table because it had no owner.
- Names and figures are exactly as the notes give them.

## Never

- Invent a decision, an owner or a date the notes do not contain.
- Include remarks that are neither decisions nor actions, such as asides about people.
